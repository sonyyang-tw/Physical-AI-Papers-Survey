### Abstract

World-Action Models (WAM) predict future states to guide robot actions, enabling learning from both action-free video and action-labeled interaction. However, most existing WAMs are built as post-training on top of pretrained video generators, leaving WAM-specific pretraining and scaling itself underexplored. This paper introduces Genie Envisioner Act 2.0 (GE-Act 2.0), whose trainable generative and action components are all initialized from scratch and trained entirely on manipulation data, rather than inheriting an existing video-generation backbone. The system combines three modules: a control-oriented autoencoder (CoAE) that retains action/instruction-relevant information under aggressive compression; a single-step visual planner (SVP) that produces a complete future state in a single differentiable forward pass; and an inverse dynamics model (IDM) that recovers actions from state transitions. The three are jointly trained via knowledge-aligned selective optimization (KASO), which selects only predicted futures judged behaviorally consistent with the recorded action, filtering out mismatched supervision. Evaluated zero-shot (no per-task fine-tuning) across 100 tasks and 20 skill groups with held-out scenes/backgrounds/lighting/object instances, scaling the co-training data from 300 to 30,000 hours raises success rate from 17.1% to 44.1% (G1-OP) and from 13.4% to 31.1% (G2-90D); despite making up less than 2% of the co-training data, G2-90D improves by 17.7 points, suggesting cross-embodiment transfer. Gains span 19/20 and 18/20 skill groups, and skill-specific coverage strongly correlates with zero-shot out-of-distribution (OOD) success (Pearson r=0.80). Under the same protocol, the model grounds object, color, shape, and position references correctly in at least 90% of trials, and follows explicit instructions even when they conflict with an already-committed behavior or a conventional scene association. Published as a technical report by the AgiBot Research Team; no formal conference acceptance has been noted yet.

### Method

**Problem addressed**: Most existing World-Action Models are built by post-training on top of a pretrained video generator (e.g., an existing video-diffusion backbone), an "inherited" approach that has left WAM-specific from-scratch pretraining and data-scaling laws largely unvalidated systematically — how much manipulation data, and what architecture, is needed for a WAM to achieve usable generalization in a zero-shot (no per-task fine-tuning) setting remains an open question.

**Main method**: GE-Act 2.0 consists of three modules trained from scratch: (1) a control-oriented autoencoder (CoAE) that aggressively compresses observations while deliberately retaining information relevant to actions and instructions, avoiding the loss of decision-relevant signal from compression; (2) a single-step visual planner (SVP) that generates a complete future state in a single differentiable forward pass, allowing visual planning and inverse dynamics modeling to be pretrained separately on complementary data; (3) an inverse dynamics model (IDM) that recovers the corresponding action from a state transition. The three modules are jointly trained via knowledge-aligned selective optimization (KASO), which filters and retains only predicted futures judged behaviorally compatible with the actually recorded action, thereby reducing training noise from mismatched supervision. Evaluation uses pretrained checkpoints directly with no per-task fine-tuning (zero-shot), tested under conditions where scenes/backgrounds/lighting/object instances are all held-out (unseen), across 100 tasks and 20 skill groups.

**Difference from prior approaches**: Unlike existing WAM lines that "inherit a pretrained video generator and then post-train," GE-Act 2.0's generative and action components are trained entirely from scratch on robot manipulation data, allowing it to directly validate the effect of "WAM data scaling" itself rather than being constrained by the prior biases or architectural limitations of an existing video-generation backbone. The decoupled CoAE/SVP/IDM design, together with the KASO selective-supervision-alignment mechanism, is a clear architectural departure from existing cascaded WAM designs or single-video-diffusion-backbone joint-denoising schemes.

### Result

**How the results turned out**: Scaling the co-training data from 300 to 30,000 hours raises the G1-OP zero-shot OOD success rate from 17.1% to 44.1%, and G2-90D from 13.4% to 31.1%; despite G2-90D data making up less than 2% of the total co-training data, it still improves by 17.7 points, showing clear cross-embodiment transfer. Gains span 19 of 20 skill groups (G1-OP) and 18 of 20 (G2-90D), and skill-specific data coverage correlates strongly with zero-shot OOD success rate (Pearson r=0.80, Spearman ρ=0.85), providing a quantitative basis for industry data-collection investment planning. Under the same protocol, the model grounds object, color, shape, and position references correctly in at least 90% of trials, and follows explicit instructions even when they conflict with scene-conventional associations or an already-committed behavior, showing consistency between language understanding and action execution.

**Are the results fair**: The evaluation covers 100 tasks across 20 skill groups, with scenes, backgrounds, lighting, and object instances all held out (unseen during training) — a strict zero-shot evaluation protocol rather than a comparison after per-task fine-tuning, which is methodologically persuasive. However, no independent third-party replication or cross-team benchmark (e.g., RoboArena) validation has been observed so far; the "data-scaling curve" and absolute success-rate figures are currently self-reported by the AgiBot team only; and compared with NVIDIA GEAR's DreamDojo (already logged on this page on 2026-09-10), which follows the same "large-scale human-data scaling" line, the two differ in data scale and evaluation protocol, so they cannot yet be directly compared to cross-validate each other.

**Main limitations**: The technical report has not yet indicated a formal conference acceptance status (it is only an AgiBot Research Team technical report), so it meets the "top-conference-caliber" criterion to a somewhat lesser degree than papers with confirmed acceptance; also, while the evaluation covers multiple skill groups, it is entirely based on AgiBot's own robot platform and data-collection pipeline, and still awaits third-party validation of whether its data-scaling law generalizes across different robot embodiments and data sources.

### Related work

Highly relevant to this page's already-covered "Robot-Specific Action-Conditioned World Models" topic are τ0-WM (arXiv:2606.01027, also from the AGIBOT team), Cosmos Policy (arXiv:2601.16163, ICLR 2026), and DreamZero (arXiv:2602.15922, led by NVIDIA), all discussing world-action-model architecture design and generalization; GE-Act 2.0 forms a direct comparison with NVIDIA GEAR's DreamDojo (arXiv:2602.06949, which similarly emphasizes large-scale data pretraining) on the "data-scaling line," worth future cross-comparison of their performance under similar evaluation protocols.

Worth surveying: Medium-high. As the direct successor to the Genie Envisioner (GE-Act) series, GE-Act 2.0, if it releases more detailed data-scaling experimental design or is validated by a third-party benchmark (e.g., RoboArena) in the future, would be an important reference point in WAM data-scaling research.

### Conclusion

**Overall assessment**: GE-Act 2.0 proposes the line of "training WAMs entirely from scratch, independent of an existing video generator," and validates via a rigorous zero-shot, held-out evaluation protocol paired with a clear data-scaling curve (Pearson r=0.80) that data scale itself directly contributes to WAM generalization ability — a technical report with solid engineering investment (100 tasks, 20 skill groups, 300-30,000 hours of data) and a clear methodology, worth referencing as an important point in WAM data-scaling research. However, since it has not yet noted a formal conference acceptance, its degree of "top-conference-caliber" is somewhat lower than the ICLR/CoRL-level papers already covered on this page; recommended to continue tracking whether it is later formally accepted at a conference or validated by a third-party benchmark.

**Relationship to other important papers**: GE-Act 2.0 is an extension of AgiBot's "Genie Envisioner" series (from the same institution as this page's already-covered τ0-WM), and forms a direct comparison group on the "data-scaling" line with NVIDIA GEAR's DreamDojo and EgoScale (which similarly emphasize large-scale data pretraining and scaling laws); it also contrasts with this page's foundational models Cosmos 3 and V-JEPA 2, general-purpose world-model lines — GE-Act 2.0's choice not to inherit a general-purpose video generator, and instead train entirely from scratch on robot data, is a design choice clearly distinct from these general-purpose world-model lines. ROCm/AMD gap: GE-Act 2.0's joint training of the CoAE/SVP/IDM three modules involves a high-throughput training pipeline for large-scale video-action data (at the 300-to-30,000-hour scale); public information currently suggests its training infrastructure is primarily NVIDIA GPU-based; if AMD wants to enter the WAM large-scale pretraining infrastructure market, it could consider validating large-scale video encoding/decoding and cross-module joint training (especially the dynamic data filtering and batch scheduling required by the KASO selective-supervision mechanism) throughput and memory efficiency on ROCm, as a concrete validation direction for entering the embodied-AI infrastructure market.
