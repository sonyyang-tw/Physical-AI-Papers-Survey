### Abstract

The ability to simulate the outcomes of actions across diverse environments will fundamentally transform the way general-purpose intelligent agents are developed. However, modeling this kind of world dynamics — especially for dexterous robotic tasks — faces major challenges due to limited data coverage and the scarcity of action labels. As an effort toward this goal, this paper proposes DreamDojo, a foundation world model that learns diverse interactions and dexterous control from 44,000 hours of egocentric human video. The authors' data mixture is by far the largest video dataset ever assembled for world model pretraining, covering a wide range of everyday scenes, diverse objects, and skills. To address the scarcity of action labels, the authors introduce continuous latent actions as a unified proxy action, strengthening the ability to transfer interaction knowledge from unlabeled video. After post-training on a small amount of target robot data, DreamDojo demonstrates strong physical understanding and precise action controllability. The authors also design a distillation pipeline that accelerates DreamDojo to a real-time speed of 10.81 frames per second, while further improving context consistency. This work enables multiple important applications built on generative world models, including live teleoperation, policy evaluation, and model-based planning. Systematic evaluation on multiple challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks validates the importance of this approach for simulating open-world, contact-rich tasks, paving the way for generalist robot world models.

### Method

**Problem addressed**: Existing robot world models rely heavily on demonstration data collected by robots themselves, which is limited in scale and scene diversity, making it difficult to support physical dynamics modeling for open-world, contact-rich tasks. At the same time, although egocentric human videos are massive in scale and scene diversity, they generally lack action labels usable directly for training. How to effectively leverage this kind of unlabeled (or weakly labeled) human data to train world models is a problem that has not yet been fully solved.

**Main method**: DreamDojo is a foundation world model trained on the largest world model pretraining dataset to date — 44,000 hours of egocentric human video (DreamDojo-HV, covering roughly 96 times the number of scenes found in the most diverse publicly available robot learning datasets). To overcome the scarcity of action labels, the authors propose "continuous latent actions" as a unified proxy action representation, allowing the model to learn transferable interaction knowledge from large amounts of video without action labels. After post-training on a small amount of target robot data, the model demonstrates precise action controllability and strong physical understanding; the authors further design a distillation pipeline that accelerates the model to a real-time speed of 10.81 FPS while improving context consistency across long rollouts.

**Difference from prior approaches**: Unlike previous world models limited by the scale of robot-collected data, DreamDojo is the first to systematically scale "egocentric human video" up to the 44K-hour order of magnitude, and uses the continuous latent action mechanism to solve the long-standing obstacle that "human video has no action labels," making it possible to transfer interaction knowledge and physics understanding from everyday human behavior. This echoes the same team's EgoScale (arXiv:2602.16710, also pushed to the VLA Papers page in this update) on the policy-learning side, but DreamDojo focuses on "prediction" rather than "directly generating actions," completing the data-scaling path on the world model side.

### Result

**Results, and which aspects were mainly enhanced**: DreamDojo validates its ability to simulate open-world, contact-rich tasks on multiple challenging OOD benchmarks; the distilled model achieves a real-time inference speed of 10.81 FPS while simultaneously improving context consistency, making real-time applications such as live teleoperation and policy evaluation — previously difficult due to latency — feasible. The scale and diversity of the authors' dataset (44K hours, roughly 96x the diversity of existing robot datasets) is itself an important contribution, going beyond a pure improvement in model performance.

**Whether the results are fair, and whether other papers' reported results conflict with this paper's results**: The paper has a large author roster spanning NVIDIA GEAR and UC Berkeley (Jitendra Malik, Pieter Abbeel), reflecting rigorous academic output; because the evaluation involves extensive self-built OOD benchmarks and human preference evaluation, the absolute comparability of the quantitative results depends on the evaluation design. No independent third party has yet been found to question or provide counterexamples to its physical understanding and controllability numbers, but since the dataset and benchmark details are not fully public, external reproduction and cross-validation remain limited.

### Limitation

**Known limitations**: One of the authors' evaluation methods is human preference evaluation, which inherently carries subjectivity and rater bias, making it less quantitatively rigorous than objective physical metrics; in addition, the model still requires post-training on "small-scale target robot data" to achieve precise controllability, indicating that pretraining on human video alone is not sufficient to directly support robot deployment — a human-to-robot "domain gap" still exists.

**Limitations and weaknesses inferred from the results**: Even after distillation reaches 10.81 FPS, this speed is still relatively low compared to lightweight VLA policies (which typically reach dozens or even hundreds of Hz), remaining a latency bottleneck when used for real-time planning or policy-in-the-loop applications; in addition, the accuracy ceiling of continuous latent actions as a proxy action is still limited by pose-estimation errors when estimating human actions from video, a type of error for which the paper does not provide sufficient quantitative analysis.

### Related work

**Whether there is newer related work on arXiv**: Closely related to this paper and published almost concurrently is EgoScale (arXiv:2602.16710, also pushed to the VLA Papers page in this update) from the same team (NVIDIA GEAR, with highly overlapping authors: Yuke Zhu, Linxi "Jim" Fan, Ruijie Zheng, et al.). That paper uses the same roughly 44K-hour-scale batch of egocentric human data to directly train a VLA policy, and validates a log-linear scaling law between data scale and validation loss. The two papers share data collection and processing infrastructure, tackling the world model (DreamDojo) and policy (EgoScale) sides respectively, and are recommended to be read together.

**Assessment of how worthwhile the related work is to survey**: Highly worthwhile. These two papers (DreamDojo + EgoScale) together mark NVIDIA GEAR's systematic investment in the research direction of "large-scale egocentric human data-driven embodied intelligence," and are of landmark significance for understanding the latest 2026 trends in world model / VLA data scaling.

### Conclusion

**Overall assessment, and whether worth referencing**: Worth referencing. DreamDojo establishes the largest human-video world model pretraining dataset to date, and proposes continuous latent actions as a concise and effective mechanism to solve the scarcity of action labels; its distillation pipeline makes real-time applications (teleoperation, policy evaluation) feasible, making it a representative work that balances world model data scaling with engineering deployment.

**Dependency map relative to other important papers, and assessment of gaps for ROCm/AMD in this area**: In terms of relationships, DreamDojo belongs to the same lineage of "large-scale generative world models" as GAIA-1/GAIA-2 (Wayve, autonomous driving world models), Genie 3 (DeepMind), and Cosmos (NVIDIA), but DreamDojo's unique positioning is using "egocentric human video rather than robot/driving data" as its pretraining source; it forms a dual-engine data-scaling pair with the same team's EgoScale (world model / policy), and also echoes GR00T N1.7 (a humanoid foundation model combining the Cosmos world model) at the technology-stack level. Gaps to be strengthened for ROCm/AMD in this area: (1) a lack of ROCm performance benchmarks and optimized operators for this kind of large-scale multimodal training workload combining "video diffusion + joint latent action modeling" (currently highly dependent on the NVIDIA CUDA/Cosmos ecosystem); (2) a lack of reference implementations and latency benchmarks for large-scale video data distillation pipelines (such as the real-time distillation in this paper) on ROCm hardware. AMD could consider prioritizing validation and optimization of "video world model inference acceleration" of this kind as an entry point into the embodied intelligence infrastructure market.
