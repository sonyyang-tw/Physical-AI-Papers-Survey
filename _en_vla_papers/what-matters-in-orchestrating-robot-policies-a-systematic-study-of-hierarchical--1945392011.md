**Paper** : [HAF: Adapting Generalist VLAs to Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-manipulation via Hierarchical Action Flow and Spectral Latent RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16837)  
**Source** : arXiv (cs.RO, cs.AI)  
**arXiv ID** : 2608.16837

### Abstract

Humanoid robots are seen as having great potential as general-purpose agents in human-centric environments, but generalist VLA foundation models cannot be directly applied to whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoids. The high dimensionality of humanoid actions and the interdependence among body parts make it difficult for traditional single-stage VLA architectures to effectively coordinate locomotion, waist posture, and dual-arm manipulation. Moreover, policies trained via offline behavior cloning may still perform poorly when deployed in the real world; while online reinforcement learning can refine policies through real-world interaction, directly fine-tuning a large VLA backbone requires extremely high computational cost and may introduce safety risks during real-robot exploration. To address these bottlenecks, the authors propose HAF (Humanoid Adaptation Framework), a framework consisting of HAF-VLA and HAF-Steer, which transfers off-the-shelf generalist VLA foundation models to whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoid robots. HAF-VLA is a hierarchical action-flow generator built on top of a pretrained flow-matching VLA, which splits the whole-body action denoising process into three sequential stages, paired with stage embeddings and cross-stage KV caching to preserve kinematic dependencies, avoiding the incoherent whole-body motion that results from one-shot generation. On top of the frozen HAF-VLA, HAF-Steer is a latent-space offline-to-online reinforcement learning pipeline that exploits the invertibility of flow matching and DCT-based dimensionality reduction to constrain RL optimization to a compact noise subspace, training a regularized SAC policy. This design avoids updating the large VLA backbone while efficiently refining the policy in the real world. In evaluations across seven real-world humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, HAF outperforms single-stage VLA baselines and improves whole-body coordination and task performance. Project website: <https://grange007.github.io/HAF>

  * Problem addressed: Although generalist VLA foundation models are powerful, they cannot be directly applied to whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoid robots, for two reasons: (1) humanoid motion is high-dimensional and different body parts (locomotion, waist posture, dual-arm manipulation) are highly interdependent, making it hard for single-stage VLA architectures to coordinate them; (2) policies trained via offline behavior cloning may be suboptimal upon real-world deployment, while directly performing online RL fine-tuning on a large VLA backbone is too costly and carries safety risks.
  * Main method: The HAF framework comprises two core components — (1) HAF-VLA: a "hierarchical action flow" generator built on top of a pretrained flow-matching VLA, which splits the denoising process of whole-body motion into three sequential stages (presumably corresponding to lower-body locomotion, torso posture, and dual-arm manipulation, though the abstract does not explicitly specify the exact correspondence of the three stages — this needs to be verified against the full text), using stage embeddings and cross-stage KV caching to preserve the kinematic dependencies between stages; (2) HAF-Steer: a latent-space offline-to-online RL pipeline layered on top of the frozen HAF-VLA, which exploits the invertibility of flow matching along with dimensionality reduction based on the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to constrain RL optimization to a compact "noise subspace," training a regularized SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) policy.
  * Difference from prior approaches: Unlike the traditional approach of "single-stage VLA generating whole-body motion in one shot," HAF-VLA adopts a staged, sequentially dependent motion-generation process to avoid the incoherent whole-body motion caused by one-shot generation; unlike the traditional approach of "directly fine-tuning the VLA backbone with online RL," HAF-Steer freezes the large VLA backbone and performs RL optimization only within a reduced-dimension, compact latent noise subspace, substantially lowering the computational cost and exploration risk of fine-tuning.
  * Key design description: The whole system can be imagined as a "generation + fine-tuning" two-layer architecture: the underlying HAF-VLA is like a staged assembly line, where motion generation flows sequentially through three processing stations, each marked with its own role via a "stage embedding," and cross-stage KV caching (similar to carrying key information from the previous station's processing forward to the next station) ensures that downstream motion remains kinematically consistent with upstream decisions, avoiding uncoordinated, awkward hand-foot motion. The upper-layer HAF-Steer is like a "calibration module" installed at the end of the assembly line — it does not alter the assembly line itself (the frozen HAF-VLA), but instead uses the invertibility of flow matching to "invert" the generation process back into a noise space, compresses this noise space into a low-dimensional subspace using DCT, and finally trains a SAC policy for real-time correction within this small space. This allows the system to leverage real-world feedback for online adjustment without bearing the cost and risk of retraining the entire large model.



### Result

  * Evaluations across seven real-world humanoid loco-manipulation tasks show that HAF outperforms "vanilla single-stage VLA baselines" and improves whole-body coordination and task performance. The abstract does not provide specific quantitative figures (e.g., success-rate percentages, magnitude of improvement); the experimental tables in the full text need to be checked to understand the specific degree of improvement.
  * Fairness: Comparison data from other papers would need to be checked; the comparison targets in the abstract are limited to "vanilla single-stage VLA baselines," and there is no direct comparison with WholeBodyVLA (also addressing whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoids) from the same batch. The two papers were published around the same time (HAF slightly later), but their abstracts do not cite each other, and whether there is a substantive performance difference needs to be verified against the full text or subsequent literature.



### Limitation

  * The abstract does not have an explicitly stated limitations section; the full text needs to be checked to understand what task types or motion complexity levels limit the framework's performance, and whether the three-stage motion-flow split is applicable to all humanoid tasks.
  * Potential weaknesses inferred from the method design: Although constraining RL optimization to a compact noise subspace lowers computational cost and safety risk for HAF-Steer, it may also limit the upper bound of freedom that the policy can explore and correct, potentially offering limited improvement for tasks that require large deviations from the pretrained distribution. In addition, the method has only been validated on seven real-world tasks — a relatively limited number — and the breadth of its generalization ability requires further verification.

### Conclusion

  * Overall assessment: HAF proposes a technically clever two-stage solution (staged action-flow generation + compact latent-space RL fine-tuning) that provides a concrete answer to the practical pain point of "large VLA backbones being difficult to fine-tune safely and efficiently online." It is worth referencing, especially for teams that want to refine policies online on real robots while avoiding retraining the entire large model.
  * Relationship to other important papers: Forms two contrasting solutions to the same problem area alongside WholeBodyVLA (arXiv:2512.11047); whether the flow-matching invertibility and DCT dimensionality-reduction technique used by HAF-Steer extends from or challenges other latent RL papers needs to be verified against the related-work section of the full text.
  * Relevance to ROCm/AMD: No clear direct connection to ROCm/AMD can be identified; the paper focuses on robot motion-generation architecture and reinforcement-learning algorithm design, without mentioning specific training/inference hardware platform choices. However, HAF-Steer's design philosophy of "avoiding updates to the large VLA backbone and performing RL only within a compact subspace" could, in theory, reduce dependence on large-scale distributed training infrastructure if deployed on AMD/ROCm platforms — though this is speculative, as the abstract itself does not provide specific details supporting this judgment.
