### Abstract

π0.7 is the third-generation general-purpose robotic foundation model from Physical Intelligence, whose core goal is "compositional generalization": enabling a single model to recombine skills learned in different contexts to solve task combinations never seen during training. Methodologically, it adopts a "diverse context conditioning" training paradigm — the model's prompt includes not only language instructions but also episode metadata describing data quality/policy, as well as multimodal signals such as sub-goal images, allowing the same model to ingest demonstration data, suboptimal autonomously collected data (including failure cases), and data from non-robot sources. The architecture is a 5B-parameter VLA: a 4B VLM backbone + an MEM-style video history encoder + an 860M-parameter action expert; at inference time, a high-level semantic policy with the same architecture generates language instructions, and a lightweight world model based on the BAGEL image-generation model produces sub-goal images. Experiments show that π0.7 can complete multi-stage tasks in unseen kitchen environments, perform zero-shot cross-embodiment manipulation (e.g., completing an unseen clothes-folding task), and, on challenging tasks such as operating an espresso machine, its out-of-the-box performance approaches that of models specifically fine-tuned with RL. The most striking case is the air fryer task: using only two fragmentary pieces of related training data, combined with half an hour of natural-language, coaching-style prompt adjustment, success rate rose from about 5% to about 95%. The paper honestly acknowledges a methodological limitation: on such a large, diverse dataset, it is hard to rigorously determine whether a task is truly "seen" or "unseen," because related skills may already exist scattered elsewhere in the dataset.

### Method

Problem addressed: Although existing VLA/robot foundation models continue to increase in scale and capability, "compositional generalization" — recombining already-learned skills to accomplish entirely new tasks — has remained difficult to achieve, which the authors regard as the core bottleneck of general-purpose embodied intelligence.

Main method: Centered on "diverse context conditioning" training, the prompt is expanded from a pure language instruction into multimodal context: language instruction + episode metadata (data quality, policy description) + sub-goal images, etc., enabling the model to be finely "steered" to complete the same task with different strategies, and thereby ingest extremely heterogeneous data sources (high-quality demonstrations, suboptimal/failed autonomous data, non-robot data). Architecturally, it is a 5B-parameter VLA: a 4B VLM backbone handles perception and language understanding, an MEM-style video history encoder processes temporal context, and an 860M-parameter action expert handles action output; at inference time, a high-level semantic policy of the same architecture generates language instructions, and a lightweight world model based on the BAGEL image-generation model produces sub-goal images, forming a division of labor of "high-level semantic planning + world-model imagined sub-goals + low-level action execution."

Difference from prior approaches: Compared with predecessors such as π0/π0.5, which condition only on language instructions plus images, π0.7 additionally introduces "policy/quality metadata" and "sub-goal images" as conditioning signals, allowing the same large, heterogeneous dataset (including suboptimal and failed trajectories) to be effectively utilized without needing to pre-filter low-quality data. It also allows human "coaching-style" natural-language prompts to adjust the policy in real time at test time (as in the air fryer case) — this kind of test-time steerability was less emphasized in previous VLA work.

### Result

Result and what it mainly improves: π0.7 performs excellently on multi-stage kitchen tasks, cross-embodiment clothes folding (an unseen task), and challenging tasks such as operating an espresso machine, achieving out-of-the-box performance close to that of specifically RL-fine-tuned models. The most representative case is the air fryer task: with only two fragmentary demonstrations related to it in the training data, half an hour of human natural-language coaching-style prompt adjustment raised the success rate from about 5% to about 95%, demonstrating very strong compositional generalization and steerability.

Whether the result is fair, and whether other papers' results conflict with this paper's: The paper is fairly candid methodologically, explicitly stating the limitation that "on a large, diverse dataset it is hard to rigorously determine whether a task is truly 'unseen'," and does not overstate the source of its generalization ability. No other paper has yet been found to directly refute or provide contradictory evidence against π0.7's specific figures, but since Physical Intelligence is the original developer of the π series, its self-reported success-rate evaluations (especially the "coaching-style prompting" experimental design, which involves real-time human intervention) still need independent third-party reproduction in an independent environment to fully rule out possible evaluation-design bias.

### Limitation

Known limitations: The authors explicitly acknowledge that on an extremely large, diverse dataset, it is in practice hard to rigorously define "whether this task truly counts as something the model has never seen" — because related skills or behavior fragments may already exist scattered throughout the massive dataset under different labels, or as part of other tasks.

Weaknesses inferred from the results: The success of the air fryer case requires human "coaching-style" language-prompt intervention in real time, which means the model's purely zero-shot generalization ability may not be as strong as the demonstration case suggests — real-world deployment may still require a human present to fine-tune the prompt to achieve a high success rate. Additionally, the evaluation scenarios were largely designed and executed by Physical Intelligence itself, lacking independent third-party reproduction and validation.

### Related work

Newer related work on arXiv: DreamZero (World Action Models are Zero-shot Policies, arXiv:2602.15922), already included on this page, as well as τ0-VLA (arXiv:2608.16885) on the VLA Papers page, both represent the latest developments in the line of "using world models/sub-goal imagination to assist high-level decision-making," and can be compared against each other to examine differences between the VLA route and the World Action Model route in terms of generalization ability. Additionally, MemoryVLA++ (arXiv:2606.09827), already included on the VLA Papers page, shares a similar problem consciousness in its "memory + imagination" design with π0.7's MEM video encoder + BAGEL world model design, and is worth studying in comparison.

Degree to which the related work is worth surveying: Worth it. DreamZero represents a completely different technical route from π0.7 (World Action Model, using a video-diffusion backbone to unify action and video prediction); the two, in 2026, constitute the core "VLA vs. WAM" route debate, and have direct reference value for AMD/ROCm in assessing which technical route to invest in for embodied intelligence.

### Conclusion

Overall assessment and whether it is worth referencing: Highly worth referencing. π0.7 comes from Physical Intelligence (the original team behind RSS 2025's π0), and is one of the most representative flagship studies in the current VLA line; its "diverse context conditioning" training paradigm and test-time steerability provide a concrete and verifiable technical path for solving VLA's long-standing lack of compositional generalization ability, making it an unavoidable reference point in embodied intelligence research in 2026.

Relationship diagram with other important papers, and areas where ROCm/AMD still need strengthening in this field: π0.7 builds upstream on π0 (arXiv:2410.24164, flow-matching continuous actions) and π0.5, and forms a route contrast with the concurrent DreamZero (World Action Model route, arXiv:2602.15922); its BAGEL-based sub-goal imagination design also intersects methodologically with MemoryVLA++ on the VLA Papers page and τ0-WM on the World Model Papers page ("world-model-assisted decision-making" research). In terms of the current state of ROCm/AMD, systems like π0.7 that require "4B VLM + 860M action expert + an additional world model" to jointly perform multi-module inference place extremely high demands on the software stack for edge/robot real-time inference (low-latency multi-model scheduling, KV-cache management, cross-module memory sharing). Currently, the ROCm ecosystem's reference implementations and performance-tuning cases for this kind of heterogeneous, multi-model real-time collaborative inference (especially closed-loop control combined with a diffusion-based world model) are noticeably fewer than in the CUDA ecosystem (e.g., NVIDIA's Cosmos/GR00T series already has mature deployment examples) — this is an area AMD needs to prioritize strengthening if it wants to enter the embodied intelligence/robotics market.
