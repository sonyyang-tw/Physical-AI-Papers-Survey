**Paper** : [Human-as-Humanoid: Enabling Zero-Shot Humanoid Learning from Ego-Exo Human Videos with Human-Aligned Embodiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32009)  
**Source** : arXiv (cs.RO)  
**arXiv ID** : 2606.32009

### Abstract

Cross-embodiment VLA models require high-quality observation-action supervision data to learn deployable action distributions, but scaling up such robot data is extremely difficult, especially for high-DoF humanoid robots. Teleoperation can provide supervision data aligned with the controller, while first-person human video, though capable of capturing diverse bimanual manipulation behaviors, cannot directly provide executable robot actions. The authors propose Human-as-Humanoid, a "human-to-humanoid" supervision framework that generates human-centric actions in near real time, enabling human demonstration data to be used for VLA training of high-DoF humanoid robots—by jointly aligning the robot embodiment, sensor setup, and action labeling interface. Built on PrimeU (a human-aligned, 60-degree-of-freedom upper-body humanoid robot), Human-as-Humanoid uses synchronized first-person-third-person (ego-exo) video, pairing "deployment-aligned first-person observations" with "third-person action recovery," retargeting the recovered human actions into controller-aligned 60-DoF action chunks via staged inverse kinematics (IK), and training the VLA model with forward-kinematics (FK)-aware supervision to preserve the task-space geometric relationships of the wrists and fingertips. This pipeline converts large-scale human demonstrations from mere visual observations into executable observation-action supervision data usable by the target humanoid robot. Experiments validate the entire conversion chain at three levels: action recovery, robot action space, and real-robot deployment. In data collection analysis, Human-as-Humanoid yields a 4.8-7.2x improvement in raw demonstration data throughput compared to humanoid teleoperation; across multiple downstream tasks, a policy post-trained using only the converted human labels generalizes to real-robot deployment without requiring any target-task robot demonstration data. Project website: <https://zgc-embodyai.github.io/Human-as-Humanoid>

  * Problem to be solved: VLA training for high-DoF humanoid robots requires large amounts of high-quality observation-action supervision data, but collecting teleoperation data is extremely costly and difficult to scale; first-person human video, while abundant and diverse in actions, lacks "executable robot action" labels that can be directly used for training—there is a gap in data form between the two.
  * Main method: Human-as-Humanoid converts human demonstration video into usable robot action supervision data by "jointly aligning the robot embodiment, sensor setup, and action labeling interface." The specific pipeline is as follows: synchronized ego (first-person) and exo (third-person) video is used, where the first-person video provides observation data from the same viewpoint as at deployment time, while the third-person video is used to precisely recover the human's 3D motion; the recovered human motion is then retargeted via staged inverse kinematics (IK) into action chunks aligned with the PrimeU (60-DoF upper-body humanoid) controller; finally, the VLA model training is supervised in a forward-kinematics (FK)-aware manner, ensuring that the task-space geometric relationships of end-effectors such as wrists and fingertips are preserved during the conversion process and not distorted by retargeting.
  * Difference from prior approaches: Unlike pure teleoperation (high cost, limited scale) or directly training with human video (lacking executable action labels, usually only usable for visual representation pretraining rather than direct action supervision), this paper is the first to enable human video to provide executable action labels "aligned with the target humanoid robot's controller"—rather than merely action references or visual priors—through the complete conversion chain of ego-exo video pairing + IK retargeting + FK-aware supervision.
  * Key method design description: The entire conversion chain can be imagined as three connected processing stations—Station 1 (observation alignment): synchronized first-person video ensures that the viewpoint seen during training matches the viewpoint seen during actual robot deployment, avoiding a gap between "what is seen during training" and "what is seen during actual deployment"; Station 2 (motion recovery and retargeting): third-person video is used to precisely estimate the human's full-body/bimanual motion, which is then mapped onto the humanoid robot's 60 degrees of freedom via staged IK (possibly solving large upper-limb joints first, then fine finger movements—the abstract does not explicitly specify the exact order of the stages, which requires verification against the full paper), forming a series of action chunks; Station 3 (FK-aware training): additional forward-kinematics supervision signals are added when training the VLA model, ensuring that the actions learned by the model, once executed, maintain wrist and fingertip spatial position relationships consistent with the original human demonstration, not merely numerically similar joint angles.

### Result

  * Main results: (1) In terms of data collection throughput, the method yields a 4.8-7.2x improvement in raw demonstration data throughput compared to humanoid teleoperation; (2) in terms of downstream tasks, a policy post-trained using only the converted human labels can generalize to real-robot deployment without requiring target-task robot demonstration data (i.e., zero-shot generalization, echoing the title); (3) experiments validate the approach at three levels: action recovery, robot action space, and real-robot deployment.
  * The abstract does not provide specific task success rate numbers or direct percentage performance comparisons with policies trained on teleoperation data; the full paper's experimental section needs to be checked to understand the specific task success rates and failure cases for "zero-shot generalization."
  * Whether the results are fair: Comparison data from other papers needs to be checked; the 4.8-7.2x throughput improvement is derived relative to "humanoid teleoperation," but the abstract does not specify the details of the comparison group's teleoperation setup (such as operator experience or hardware configuration)—whether this is a fair comparison requires checking the experimental setup details in the full paper.

### Limitation

  * The abstract does not contain an explicit limitations section; the full paper needs to be checked to understand for which action types (e.g., high-speed motions, fine force-control tasks) the framework might suffer retargeting distortion due to differences between human and robot embodiments.
  * Potential weaknesses inferred from the method design: the entire pipeline is highly dependent on the specific design of PrimeU, a "human-aligned" 60-DoF upper-body humanoid robot; for humanoid robots whose body structure differs significantly from humans (e.g., fewer degrees of freedom or different joint configurations), the transferability of this retargeting and FK-aware supervision method may be limited. Additionally, the method only covers the upper body (arms + hands) and does not mention lower-body locomotion capability, so it may not directly handle whole-body tasks requiring movement (in contrast to the loco-manipulation problems focused on by WholeBodyVLA and HAF in this batch).

### Related work

  * Shares a similar spirit with WholeBodyVLA (arXiv:2512.11047) from the same batch—WholeBodyVLA also proposes a unified latent learning framework for "learning from low-cost, action-label-free first-person video," and both attempt to address the problem of "scarcity of humanoid robot action data." However, Human-as-Humanoid focuses more on the data conversion chain itself—"how to convert human video into precise, executable action labels"—while WholeBodyVLA focuses more on the control problem of "locomotion + manipulation coordination." The two can be viewed as complementary rather than directly competing.
  * No direct follow-up work newer than this paper has been found so far (this paper was submitted in June 2026).
  * Moderately-to-highly worth surveying as related work: for any team looking to reduce the cost of humanoid robot data collection, the ego-exo + IK + FK supervision chain method proposed in this paper is valuable, and it is recommended to track and compare it alongside WholeBodyVLA and the broader literature on "learning robot actions from human video."

### Conclusion

  * Overall assessment: This is a paper that proposes a concrete, fully engineered solution to the key bottleneck of "humanoid robot data scarcity." Through a clever ego-exo video pairing and kinematic retargeting design, it converts human video—originally unusable directly—into executable action supervision data. The throughput improvement and zero-shot generalization results are quite convincing, and this paper is worth referencing.
  * Relationship with other important papers: Along with WholeBodyVLA (arXiv:2512.11047), this belongs to related research addressing the problem of "humanoid robot data scarcity," but with a different entry point (this paper emphasizes the precision of the data conversion pipeline, while WholeBodyVLA emphasizes controller design for locomotion-manipulation coordination); it is also directly related to the "data fidelity-cost trade-off" issue mentioned in the VLA survey (2604.23001) from the same batch, and can be seen as a concrete attempt to solve that trade-off (replacing some expensive teleoperation data with retargeted human data).
  * Regarding ROCm/AMD: No clear direct connection to ROCm/AMD is apparent; the paper focuses on the data conversion pipeline (computer-vision-based motion recovery, inverse/forward kinematics) and VLA training supervision design, without mentioning specific training/inference hardware platforms or accelerator choices, so it is not possible to judge the specific needs or contribution opportunities this field presents for the ROCm ecosystem.
