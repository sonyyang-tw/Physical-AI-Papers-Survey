### Abstract

DreamZero is a World Action Model (WAM) led by NVIDIA (with authors including Linxi "Jim" Fan, Jan Kautz, Yuke Zhu, among others). Its core claim is that although current state-of-the-art VLA models excel at semantic-level generalization, they struggle to generalize to "unseen physical actions" in new environments. DreamZero is built on a pretrained video diffusion backbone (Wan2.1-I2V-14B-480P, a 14B-parameter image-to-video diffusion model). By jointly modeling video and action—treating video as a dense representation of "how the world evolves"—it effectively learns diverse skills from heterogeneous robot data without relying on large amounts of repetitive demonstrations. Real-robot experiments show that, compared to state-of-the-art VLAs, DreamZero achieves more than a 2x improvement in generalization to new tasks and new environments; through system- and model-level optimizations, the team successfully brought a 14B-parameter autoregressive video diffusion model up to 7Hz real-time closed-loop control. The paper also demonstrates two forms of cross-embodiment transfer: using only 10-20 minutes of demonstration video from other robots or humans, it achieves more than a 42% relative performance improvement on unseen tasks; even more surprisingly, DreamZero requires only 30 minutes of play data from a new robot (YAM) to achieve few-shot embodiment adaptation while retaining its zero-shot generalization ability. On the April 2026 RoboArena leaderboard, DreamZero (a joint-prediction WAM based on Wan 2.1-I2V-14B) achieved an Elo of 1750, ahead of π0.5's 1622 and π-FAST's 1592, making it one of the most representative empirical results in the WAM line of work to date.

### Method

Problem being addressed: existing VLAs (such as the π series, OpenVLA, etc.) perform excellently at semantic generalization (understanding new instructions, new object names) but perform poorly at "physical action generalization in new environments"—in other words, the model can understand new tasks but does not necessarily execute the corresponding physical action sequence correctly.

Main method: unlike VLAs that treat action generation as a downstream module independent of visual understanding, DreamZero has the same video diffusion backbone jointly denoise-predict both "actions" and "future video frames" (joint video-action modeling), internalizing action generation as part of the video generation process itself. This allows the model to learn physical dynamics directly from video data (including large amounts of human/other-robot video without action annotations), without relying on large amounts of repetitive robot demonstration data. To enable the 14B-parameter autoregressive video diffusion model to achieve 7Hz real-time closed-loop control, the paper performs targeted optimizations at the system level, implementation level, and model level (i.e., DreamZero-Flash).

Difference from prior approaches: compared to traditional VLAs that treat VLM understanding and action decoding as two stages (or even two separate modules), DreamZero unifies the two under the perspective of "video as dense world representation"—video prediction itself implicitly involves understanding of physical dynamics, and action is just one dimension of this prediction process. This makes cross-embodiment transfer more natural: as long as there is video (whether captured by humans or other robots), it can serve as a training signal, without requiring that video to carry action annotations matching the target robot.

### Result

Results and what was primarily enhanced: compared to state-of-the-art VLAs, DreamZero achieves more than a 2x improvement in generalization to new tasks/environments; through cross-embodiment transfer via video demonstrations (human or other robot), it achieves more than a 42% relative performance improvement on unseen tasks using only 10-20 minutes of data; using only 30 minutes of play data, it can achieve few-shot adaptation to a completely new robot embodiment (YAM). It ranked first on the public RoboArena leaderboard (April 2026) with 1750 Elo, clearly ahead of π0.5 (1622) and π-FAST (1592).

Is the result fair, and do other papers' results conflict with this paper's results: RoboArena is a third-party-maintained public evaluation leaderboard, so DreamZero's ranking on it (rather than merely an internally self-reported evaluation by the authors) carries a certain degree of credibility and comparability, which is more convincing than pure self-reported success rates. The project has open-sourced the model weights, inference code, and the evaluation code used on RoboArena, PolaRiS, and Genie Sim 3.0, allowing third-party reproduction, which gives it relatively high transparency. So far, no other paper has been found to directly refute its RoboArena ranking.

### Limitation

Known limitations: the paper self-reports that on high-precision manipulation tasks requiring "sub-millimeter precision" (such as peg insertion and precision assembly), DreamZero inherits the general limitations of behavior cloning methods—its diverse pretraining strategy emphasizes "breadth," which may underrepresent the dense demonstration data required for high-precision manipulation. The authors also cite a contemporaneous study (Kim et al., 2026) suggesting that the WAM approach may actually have an advantage on millimeter-level precision tasks, implying that the trade-off between "broad generalization" and "fine manipulation" is not necessarily irreconcilable, though this has not been verified within DreamZero's own experiments.

Limitations and weaknesses inferred from the results: even with extensive system-level optimization, the 14B-parameter video diffusion model barely reaches 7Hz closed-loop control, which is still a significant bottleneck for practical deployment compared to traditional lightweight VLAs (which can reach dozens of Hz), especially for fine manipulation tasks that require high-frequency feedback correction.

### Related work

Newer related work on arXiv: this paper belongs to the same "joint video-action modeling" line of work as τ0-WM (arXiv:2606.01027, AGIBOT) and VLAW (arXiv:2602.12063), already included on this World Model Papers page, allowing comparison of implementation differences between different teams (NVIDIA vs. AGIBOT) pursuing this design approach. In addition, "World Action Models: The Next Frontier in Embodied AI" (arXiv:2605.12090), included on the VLA Papers page, explicitly discusses DreamZero-style Joint WAMs alongside Cascaded WAMs, and can serve as supplementary reading for understanding where this paper sits within the overall WAM taxonomy. π0.7 (arXiv:2604.15483, Physical Intelligence, already included on the VLA Papers page), which appeared around the same time, represents another solution to the same generalization problem from the "VLA camp"; the two papers form the most important line-of-work comparison in embodied AI in 2026.

Assessment of how worth surveying the related work is: very much so. DreamZero and π0.7 respectively represent the flagship achievements of the WAM and VLA camps in the first half of 2026, and they give strikingly different answers to "how to achieve generalizable embodied intelligence." Studying them side by side helps clarify the strengths, weaknesses, and applicable scenarios of each approach.

### Conclusion

Overall assessment—is it worth referencing: very much worth referencing. DreamZero comes from a large NVIDIA team (including well-known researchers Linxi "Jim" Fan and Jan Kautz) and achieved a verified first-place result on the third-party RoboArena leaderboard, making it one of the most compelling empirical studies of the World Action Model approach to date, and highly valuable for assessing the viability of the "video diffusion backbone + joint action modeling" technical route.

Relationship to other important papers and remaining gaps for ROCm/AMD in this field: DreamZero is built on the Wan2.1-I2V-14B video diffusion backbone, forming a technical lineage with τ0-WM (AGIBOT) and VLAW, which belong to the same WAM line of work; it also forms a cross-line comparison with π0.7 from the VLA camp and other baselines on this page (GAIA-2, V-JEPA 2, and other video world models). Regarding the current state of ROCm/AMD, work like DreamZero—which requires optimizing a 14B-parameter autoregressive video diffusion model down to 7Hz real-time closed-loop control—relies heavily on three-tiered optimization (system-level, implementation-level, and model-level) specific to video diffusion models (such as KV-cache reuse, accelerated stepwise denoising, and mixed-precision scheduling). NVIDIA, leveraging deep hardware co-optimization through its Cosmos series, has already established a clear first-mover advantage in this kind of "large video generation model for real-time robot control" scenario, while the ROCm ecosystem currently lacks corresponding public examples and performance benchmarks for real-time inference optimization of video diffusion models (particularly low-latency closed-loop control under autoregressive architectures)—this is a software stack capability that AMD would need to prioritize building in order to gain a foothold in the WAM line of embodied intelligence competition.
