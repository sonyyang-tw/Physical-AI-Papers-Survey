### Abstract

Recent video generation models have demonstrated a strong ability to capture complex physical interactions and scene evolution, prompting robotics researchers to attempt to repurpose video models as policies. However, existing approaches often require multi-stage post-training and additional action-generation architectural components, adding unnecessary complexity. This paper proposes Cosmos Policy, a concise method for converting a large pretrained video model (Cosmos-Predict2) into an effective robot policy: it requires only a single stage of post-training on demonstration data collected on the target platform, with no architectural modifications whatsoever. Cosmos Policy encodes robot actions as "latent frames" generated directly within the video model's latent diffusion process, fully leveraging the model's existing pretrained priors and learning algorithms to capture complex action distributions. In addition, the model generates future state images and values (expected cumulative reward) in the same manner, enabling test-time planning over action trajectories at inference time to improve success probability. On the LIBERO and RoboCasa simulation benchmarks, Cosmos Policy achieves average success rates of 98.5% and 67.1% respectively, setting new state-of-the-art records; on a challenging real-world bimanual manipulation task, its average score also surpasses a strong diffusion policy trained from scratch, existing video-model policies, and a state-of-the-art VLA model fine-tuned on the same robot demonstration data. Furthermore, given policy rollout data, Cosmos Policy can continuously refine its world model and value function from experience, and use model-based planning to achieve higher success rates on difficult tasks. This paper has been accepted to ICLR 2026.

### Method

**Problem addressed:** Transferring the spatiotemporal priors of pretrained video models to robot policy learning is a promising route to improved generalization and data efficiency, but existing methods typically require introducing new action-generation architecture (e.g., an additional action head or diffusion policy head) and splitting the process into multi-stage post-training (first fine-tuning for video prediction, then training an action decoder). This makes the overall system complex and hard to maintain, and may fail to fully exploit the priors already learned by the video model.

**Main method:** The core innovation of Cosmos Policy is "latent frame injection": without modifying the architecture of the Cosmos-Predict2 video diffusion model, robot actions, future states (proprioception + image observations), and value estimates are all encoded as "latent frames" within the model's sequence structure, jointly modeled together with the original image/action sequence under the same video diffusion training objective. In other words, action generation, future prediction, and value evaluation are all reformulated as the problem of "generating the next batch of latent frames," so the model can directly reuse the video model's existing training algorithm and pretrained weights, requiring only a single stage of post-training on robot demonstration data to adapt. At inference time, the model not only outputs action sequences but simultaneously generates corresponding future state images and value estimates, allowing the system to plan over multiple candidate action trajectories at test time and select the one with the highest expected value for execution.

**Difference from prior approaches:** Compared with the typical "video model + additional action head + multi-stage training" paradigm, Cosmos Policy introduces no new architectural components at all; through the representation-level insight of "latent frame injection" alone, the same video diffusion backbone simultaneously handles action generation, future prediction, and value evaluation, and the training pipeline is simplified to a single stage. This allows it to more fully preserve and exploit the priors of the pretrained video model, while also enabling test-time planning via the generated value estimates — a capability not present in existing VLA or video-model-policy approaches.

### Result

Cosmos Policy achieves a 98.5% average success rate on the LIBERO simulation benchmark and a 67.1% average success rate across 24 RoboCasa kitchen manipulation tasks, both state-of-the-art. In real-world evaluations on the ALOHA bimanual robot, Cosmos Policy's average score surpasses a strong diffusion policy trained from scratch, existing video-model policies, and state-of-the-art VLA models such as π0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT+ fine-tuned on the same batch of robot demonstration data; the paper also presents specific failure modes of π0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT+ on fine-grained, long-horizon tasks as a point of comparison. In addition, the authors demonstrate that, given policy rollout data, Cosmos Policy can continuously refine its world model and value function using its own generated experience, and further improve success rates on difficult tasks via model-based planning, showing that its latent-frame architecture has closed-loop, self-improving potential. Because the evaluation spans both public simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa) and real hardware (ALOHA), and is directly compared against multiple publicly published strong baselines (diffusion policy, π0.5, etc.), the results carry a relatively high degree of fairness and credibility.

### Limitation

The paper focuses on verifying the method's effectiveness and discusses limitations relatively briefly, but plausible weaknesses include: (1) the model is highly dependent on the quality and priors of the specific pretrained video foundation model Cosmos-Predict2; if transferred to other video model backbones, the effectiveness of the latent frame injection mechanism remains to be verified; (2) real-world evaluation is limited to the ALOHA bimanual platform, and systematic verification of transferability across embodiments (e.g., humanoid, single-arm, quadruped) is not shown; (3) video diffusion models typically have higher inference latency than lightweight VLA policies, and although the paper demonstrates efficiency gains from test-time planning, it does not sufficiently discuss the impact on real-time deployment latency/throughput, which could be a deployment bottleneck for contact-rich tasks requiring high-frequency control.

### Related work

Cosmos Policy belongs to the same technical track as the Robot-Specific Action-Conditioned World Models category already covered on this page (e.g., Ctrl-World, τ0-WM, WEAVER) — namely, "coupling world model generative capability with robot actions." However, Cosmos Policy's distinctive feature is that it does not design a separate, independent world model for the policy to query; instead, it directly unifies action generation, state prediction, and value estimation as latent-frame generation tasks within the same video diffusion model, resulting in a more streamlined architecture. In addition, Cosmos Policy shares a common philosophy with NVIDIA's contemporaneous DreamZero (arXiv:2602.15922, already included in this page's 2026-09-09 update log) — "video as dense world representation" — but DreamZero focuses on zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization and validation on the RoboArena leaderboard, while Cosmos Policy focuses more on integrating test-time planning with value estimation. The two can be seen as complementary attempts by NVIDIA along the "video model as policy" track, worth tracking together to see how their technical directions converge.

### Conclusion

Through the elegant and concise "latent frame injection" mechanism, Cosmos Policy demonstrates that a pretrained video model can simultaneously serve as an action generator, future predictor, and value estimator with almost no increase in architectural complexity, achieving top-tier results in both simulation and real-world evaluations. It is an important work in the World-Action Model track that combines methodological simplicity with strong empirical evidence, and is well worth prioritizing as a reference. In terms of its relationship to other important papers, Cosmos Policy, together with DreamZero, τ0-WM, and others, outlines the emerging convergence trend of "video models gradually replacing/merging with traditional VLA action heads." Regarding AMD/ROCm, Cosmos Policy is built directly on top of NVIDIA's Cosmos-Predict2, a video foundation model deeply tied to the CUDA ecosystem, highlighting a significant gap in ROCm's current capability for "efficient training and inference of large-scale video diffusion foundation models." To reproduce or improve upon this "video model as policy" research direction on AMD hardware, one would first need to fill the gap with open-source video diffusion foundation models of comparable scale and quality, along with corresponding ROCm-optimized training/inference frameworks — a critical piece of infrastructure that the AMD/ROCm ecosystem urgently needs to strengthen at this stage.
