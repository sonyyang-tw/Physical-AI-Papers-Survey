### Abstract

Robotic foundation models still need task-specific fine-tuning before deployment, and the fine-tuned policies often break under modest changes in scene layout, lighting, or nearby distractors. The paper traces this brittleness to shortcut learning: fine-tuning supervises actions only, not the visual evidence the policy uses, so the policy can settle on scene-level correlations (background texture, lighting, object co-occurrence, camera artifacts) that predict the demonstrations without causing success. The authors propose Artificial Foveated Perception (AFP), a lightweight, policy-agnostic module that takes the same vision and language inputs as existing VLA/World Action Model pipelines and predicts task-conditioned masks over the relevant objects, the robot, and other action-critical regions. During fine-tuning the masks serve as an auxiliary grounding signal that aligns the policy's visual attention with task-relevant regions; the policy architecture is unchanged, and at inference the policy runs on the original observation stream with no AFP call in the control loop. In simulation with four robotic foundation models (SmolVLA, OpenVLA, π0.5, Motus) across eight MimicGen tasks, and on a real robot with π0.5 across five tasks (from FurnitureBench and custom scenes), AFP improves generalization under environmental perturbations, reduces overfitting, and shortens fine-tuning on long-horizon tasks. Ablations over mask quality and grounding-loss design confirm these gains come from directing policy learning toward task-relevant visual evidence. Accepted at CoRL 2026.

### Method

**Problem addressed**: The fine-tuning pipeline for robotic foundation models supervises only the action labels, with no constraint on which visual cues the policy relies on; the policy can therefore learn a "shortcut" — a scene-level correlation (background texture, lighting conditions, object co-occurrence, camera artifacts) that happens to correlate with demonstrated actions in the training scene but has no causal contribution to task success. Such shortcuts perform normally within the training distribution but fail immediately once scene layout, lighting, or distractor objects perturb the environment.

**Main method**: AFP is a standalone, lightweight, policy-agnostic task-conditioned mask predictor. Its architecture has two paths: (1) a Feature path — a MobileNetV3-Large backbone yields a multi-scale feature pyramid, each level projected and flattened into visual tokens fed into a deformable transformer encoder; (2) a Query path — a small number of learnable region queries decode against the visual memory via deformable cross-attention, acting as compact descriptors of task-relevant objects and the end-effector, with temporal modules keeping masks stable across frames. Language conditioning is applied via a frozen CLIP text embedding driving a FiLM adapter that performs scale/shift/gate modulation on the decoded queries (rather than concatenating text as tokens). A mask head fuses the multi-scale features into a high-resolution map, matching the primary query against every spatial location and applying a sigmoid to yield a continuous mask M_t ∈ [0,1]^(H×W). During fine-tuning, the policy's attention over image tokens is averaged across trainable layers/heads/query positions and compared with the pooled AFP mask, both normalized, to compute a grounding loss L_AFP, and the policy is trained jointly with L = L_act + λ·L_AFP. To avoid conflicting gradients between the grounding signal and action learning, a PCGrad-like projected-gradient mechanism is used: when the AFP gradient points against the action gradient, the conflicting component is removed before updating (11,152 projections were triggered over 20k fine-tuning steps in the experiments; without this mechanism, training loss spikes sharply past the halfway point of training).

**Difference from prior approaches**: Existing approaches for integrating tactile/vision multimodal signals into a policy, or existing VLA/WAM fine-tuning paradigms, all supervise only at the action level, with no mechanism to constrain or correct which visual evidence the policy relies on when producing an action. AFP is the first to reframe "aligning policy visual attention during fine-tuning" as a standalone auxiliary supervision problem, and is deliberately designed to be entirely absent at inference time (zero inference-time overhead) without modifying the policy's own architecture — a clear departure from approaches that require modifying the policy architecture or introducing extra computation into the control loop (e.g., modules that must be called continuously within the control loop).

### Result

**How the results turned out**: Across eight simulated MimicGen tasks and four foundation models (SmolVLA, OpenVLA, π0.5, Motus), AFP raises π0.5's in-distribution Soft-IoU from 0.17 (direct fine-tuning) to 0.93, and lowers EMD from 4.39 to 0.04, showing attention is indeed pulled back to human-labeled task-relevant regions; out-of-distribution (with 3-5 unseen RoboSuite objects added as distractors), success rate improves across the board, while in-distribution AFP at least matches or exceeds direct fine-tuning (no loss of original performance). Real-world validation with π0.5 on an i2RT YAM Arm across five tasks (including FurnitureBench's Drawer/Lamp/Table Assembly, Cloth Folding, Closet Storage) shows AFP improving across the board in both distributions, with the largest gains out-of-distribution. Furthermore, on long-horizon, multi-stage tasks (Kitchen, Coffee Preparation, Three Piece Assembly), AFP-trained policies reach high success rates noticeably faster, with less pronounced gains on short tasks — suggesting the gain correlates with the proportion of the observation stream that is incidental to the task.

**Are the results fair**: The paper validates across four architecturally distinct foundation models (different action-head designs including autoregressive and diffusion/flow-matching) in both simulation and real-world dual-track evaluation, with ablations (mask quality, grounding-loss design, with/without projected gradients) systematically decomposing the source of the gains — a reasonable degree of scientific rigor. The project website and paper both provide code, an annotation tool, and a dataset (on Hugging Face) for third-party reproduction. No other paper has yet directly refuted this method or reported inconsistent results, though it is newly published and has not yet undergone extensive third-party replication.

### Limitation

**Known limitations**: AFP depends on human-annotated task-relevance masks for training — requiring humans to annotate point prompts on a small number of key frames, then propagate via SAM 2 + MatAnyone across the sequence, incurring additional annotation cost and potential labeling bias. AFP can fail when task relevance itself is ambiguous, objects are heavily occluded, or the scene falls outside the distribution of the mask dataset.

**Weaknesses inferred from results**: The two extension directions the paper acknowledges (scaling the annotation dataset, or learning relevance automatically from interaction/temporal consistency/counterfactual scene changes/policy failures instead of relying on human annotation) themselves suggest the current method still has clear bottlenecks in data scaling and annotation automation; real-world validation is also limited to a single robotic-arm platform (i2RT YAM Arm) and five tasks, with cross-embodiment transferability still to be broadly validated.

### Related work

Related existing work on arXiv highly relevant to this direction includes general research on shortcut learning in visual representation learning, and existing attention-alignment/grounding auxiliary-loss methods; Sparse Autoencoders Reveal Interpretable and Steerable Features in VLA Models (2603.19183) and Not All Features Are Created Equal (2603.19233), already covered on this page's VLA "Interpretability & Diagnostics" topic, analyze the mechanistic behavior of VLA's internal visual/language pathways, offering a complementary perspective to AFP's investigation of "what visual evidence the policy relies on" — worth cross-referencing.

Worth surveying: Medium-high. The "align attention during fine-tuning, zero extra overhead at inference" paradigm AFP proposes has cross-architecture, cross-embodiment transfer potential, and has released complete code, an annotation tool, and a dataset; future work validating or extending it to more foundation models/real platforms would be worth continued tracking.

### Conclusion

**Overall assessment**: AFP's problem diagnosis (shortcut learning arising because fine-tuning supervises only actions, not visual evidence) is precise and generalizable, and its solution design is elegant (zero inference-time overhead, policy-agnostic), validated thoroughly across four representative foundation models in a simulation-plus-real-world dual track, while also open-sourcing code, an annotation tool, and a dataset — a CoRL 2026 paper worth referencing, especially valuable for any team fine-tuning robotic foundation models for real-world deployment.

**Relationship to other important papers**: AFP shares with this page's "Interpretability & Diagnostics" topic (Sparse Autoencoders, Not All Features Are Created Equal, Decoding Task Progress, Tri-Info) a concern for understanding/diagnosing VLA's internal visual-language pathways, but goes further by turning diagnostic insight into a training-time intervention that directly improves downstream generalization; it is also directly relevant to this page's foundational model π0.5 (arXiv:2604.15483), since AFP's real-robot experiments use π0.5 as one of its validation backbones. ROCm/AMD gap: AFP's training and inference currently default to an NVIDIA GPU ecosystem (PyTorch + CUDA-accelerated deformable attention/transformer computation), with no ROCm-specific deformable-attention operator optimization or cross-platform benchmarking observed yet; since AFP itself has zero extra inference-time overhead and only introduces computational load during training, if AMD wants to enter the robotic foundation model fine-tuning infrastructure market, it could consider validating the training performance and numerical stability of deformable transformers + FiLM language modulation on ROCm, as a concrete entry point for further ecosystem expansion.
