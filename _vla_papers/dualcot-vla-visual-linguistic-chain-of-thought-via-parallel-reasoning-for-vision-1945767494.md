**Paper** : [Human-as-Humanoid: Enabling Zero-Shot Humanoid Learning from Ego-Exo Human Videos with Human-Aligned Embodiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32009)  
**Source** : arXiv (cs.RO)  
**arXiv ID** : 2606.32009

### Abstract

VLA models across robot embodiments require high-quality observation-action supervision data to learn deployable action distributions, but scaling up this kind of robot data is extremely difficult, especially for high-degree-of-freedom (high-DoF) humanoid robots. Teleoperation can provide controller-aligned supervision data, and human egocentric video, while capturing diverse bimanual manipulation actions, cannot directly provide executable robot actions. The authors propose Human-as-Humanoid, a "human-to-humanoid" supervision framework that generates human-centric actions in near real time, allowing human demonstration data to be used for VLA training of high-DoF humanoid robots — by jointly aligning the robot embodiment, sensing setup, and action-labeling interface. This framework is built on top of PrimeU (a human-aligned 60-DoF upper-body humanoid robot). Human-as-Humanoid uses synchronized egocentric-exocentric (ego-exo) video, pairing "deployment-aligned egocentric observations" with "exocentric action recovery," retargeting recovered human actions into controller-aligned 60-DoF action chunks through staged inverse kinematics (IK), and training the VLA model with forward-kinematics (FK)-aware supervision to preserve the task-space geometric relationships of the wrist and fingertips. This pipeline converts large-scale human demonstrations from mere visual observation into executable observation-action supervision data usable by a target humanoid robot. Experiments validate the entire conversion chain at three levels: action recovery, robot action space, and real-robot deployment. In the data-collection analysis, Human-as-Humanoid brings a 4.8-7.2x improvement in raw demonstration data throughput compared to humanoid teleoperation; across multiple downstream tasks, policies post-trained using only the converted human labels can generalize to real-robot deployment without needing target-task robot demonstration data. Project website: <https://zgc-embodyai.github.io/Human-as-Humanoid>

### Method

_Figure 2(a): Illustration of the Visual CoT. Learnable spatial query vectors decode the compressed output hidden states of the visual CoT tokens into high-resolution feature maps via cross-attention, with frozen DA3 dense features serving as the supervision signal for the MSE loss._

  * Problem addressed: VLA training for high-DoF humanoid robots requires large amounts of high-quality observation-action supervision data, but collecting teleoperation data is extremely costly and difficult to scale up; human egocentric video, while abundant in quantity and diverse in action, lacks "executable robot action" labels directly usable for training, creating a data-format gap between the two.
  * Main method: Human-as-Humanoid converts human demonstration video into usable robot-action supervision data by "jointly aligning the robot embodiment, sensing setup, and action-labeling interface." The specific pipeline is: using synchronously captured ego (egocentric) and exo (exocentric) video, where the egocentric video provides observation data from the same viewpoint as at deployment time, while the exocentric video is used to precisely recover the human's 3D actions; the recovered human actions are then retargeted via staged inverse kinematics (IK) into action chunks aligned with the PrimeU (60-DoF upper-body humanoid) controller; finally, the VLA model training is supervised in a forward-kinematics (FK)-aware manner, ensuring that the task-space geometric relationships of end-effectors such as the wrist and fingertips are preserved during the conversion process and are not distorted by retargeting.
  * Difference from prior approaches: Unlike pure teleoperation (costly, limited scalability) or directly training on human video (lacking executable action labels, usually usable only for visual representation pretraining rather than direct action supervision), this paper is the first to enable human video to provide executable action labels "aligned with the target humanoid robot's controller" — rather than merely action references or visual priors — through a complete conversion chain of ego-exo video pairing + IK retargeting + FK-aware supervision.
  * Description of the key methodological design: The entire conversion chain can be envisioned as three connected processing stations — Station 1 (observation alignment): synchronized egocentric video ensures that the viewpoint seen during training matches the viewpoint the robot sees at deployment, avoiding a gap between "the frames seen during training" and "the frames seen during actual deployment"; Station 2 (action recovery and retargeting): exocentric video is used to precisely estimate the human's whole-body/bimanual actions, which are then mapped onto the humanoid robot's 60 degrees of freedom via staged IK (possibly first solving the large upper-body joints, then the fine finger movements — the abstract does not specify the exact order of the stages, which needs to be verified in the full text), forming successive action chunks; Station 3 (FK-aware training): when training the VLA model, an additional forward-kinematics supervision signal is added, ensuring that the actions learned by the model, after execution, have the same spatial relationships for the wrist and fingertips as in the original human demonstration, not merely similar joint-angle values.

### Result

  * Main results: (1) In terms of data-collection throughput, it brings a 4.8-7.2x improvement in raw demonstration data throughput compared to humanoid teleoperation; (2) In terms of downstream tasks, policies post-trained using only the converted human labels can generalize to real-robot deployment without needing target-task robot demonstration data (i.e., zero-shot generalization, echoing the title); (3) Experiments validate all three levels — action recovery, robot action space, and real-robot deployment.
  * The abstract does not provide specific task success-rate numbers or direct percentage comparisons of effectiveness against policies trained on teleoperation data; the full text's experiment section needs to be checked for the specific task success rates and failure cases of "zero-shot generalization."
  * Fairness: comparative data from other papers needs to be verified; the 4.8-7.2x throughput improvement is derived by comparison with "humanoid teleoperation," but the abstract does not describe the specific teleoperation setup of the control group (e.g., operator experience, hardware configuration); whether this is a fair comparison needs to be verified against the experimental setup details in the full text.

### Limitation

  * The abstract does not contain an explicitly self-stated limitation section; the full text needs to be checked to understand which action types (e.g., high-speed actions, fine force-control tasks) this framework may distort through retargeting due to differences between human and robot embodiments.
  * Potential weaknesses inferred from the methodological design: the entire pipeline relies heavily on the specific "human-aligned" 60-DoF upper-body humanoid design of PrimeU; for humanoid robots with embodiment structures more different from humans (e.g., fewer degrees of freedom, different joint configurations), the transferability of this retargeting and FK-aware supervision method may be limited; furthermore, the method only covers the upper body (arms + hands) and does not mention lower-body locomotion capability, so it may not directly handle whole-body tasks requiring movement (in contrast to the loco-manipulation problems focused on by WholeBodyVLA and HAF from the same batch).

### Related work

  * This paper shares a common spirit with WholeBodyVLA (arXiv:2512.11047) from the same batch — WholeBodyVLA also proposes a unified latent-learning framework for "learning from low-cost, action-label-free egocentric video"; both attempt to address the problem of "scarce humanoid robot action data," but Human-as-Humanoid focuses more on the data-conversion chain itself — "how to convert human video into precise executable action labels" — while WholeBodyVLA focuses more on the control problem of "coordinating locomotion + manipulation"; the two can be seen as complementary rather than directly competing.
  * No direct follow-up research newer than this paper has been found so far (this paper was submitted in June 2026).
  * Degree to which the related work merits surveying: moderately high. For any team wanting to reduce the cost of humanoid robot data collection, the ego-exo + IK + FK supervision chain method proposed in this paper has reference value; it is recommended to track and compare it alongside WholeBodyVLA and the broader literature on "learning robot actions from human video."

### Conclusion

  * Overall assessment: This is a paper that proposes a concrete, engineering-detail-complete solution to the key bottleneck of "scarce humanoid robot data." Through clever ego-exo video pairing and kinematic retargeting design, it converts human video that could not previously be used directly into executable action supervision data; the throughput improvement and zero-shot generalization results are quite persuasive and worth referencing.
  * Relationship to other important papers: it belongs to the same research area as WholeBodyVLA (arXiv:2512.11047) in addressing "scarce humanoid robot data," but with a different entry point (this paper emphasizes the precision of the data-conversion pipeline, while WholeBodyVLA emphasizes controller design for locomotion-manipulation coordination); it is also directly related to the "data fidelity-cost trade-off" issue mentioned in the VLA survey from the same batch (2604.23001), and this paper can be viewed as a concrete attempted solution to that trade-off problem (replacing part of the expensive teleoperation data with retargeted human data).
  * ROCm/AMD relevance: no clear direct connection to ROCm/AMD is apparent. The paper focuses on the data-conversion pipeline (computer-vision action recovery, inverse/forward kinematics) and VLA training supervision design, without mentioning specific training/inference hardware platforms or accelerator choices, so it is impossible to judge this field's specific needs for or contribution opportunities to the ROCm ecosystem.
